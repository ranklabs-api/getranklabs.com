# The Schema Markup Playbook: How Structured Data Gets You Cited by AI

Most businesses are still optimizing for blue links. Meanwhile, AI engines are making decisions about who to cite based on something completely different: structured data.

If your site doesn't have schema markup, you're invisible to the algorithms that power ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Perplexity.

## What Is Schema Markup?

Schema markup is code you add to your website that tells search engines exactly what your content means — not just what it says. It's the difference between an AI seeing "we help businesses grow" and an AI seeing "this is a LocalBusiness named Rank Labs, located in Florida, offering SEO and GEO services, rated 4.9 stars."

Schema.org defines over 800 types. The ones that matter for AI visibility are:

- **LocalBusiness** — name, address, phone, hours, services, geo-coordinates
- **Organization** — brand name, logo, social profiles, founding date
- **FAQ** — question-and-answer pairs that AI can extract directly
- **Article** — headline, author, date published, publisher
- **Product** — name, description, price, availability, reviews
- **Review** — aggregate ratings and individual reviews

Without these, AI engines have to guess what your business is. With them, there's no guessing — the data is structured, explicit, and machine-readable.

## Why Schema Markup Matters for AI Search

AI engines don't "browse" your site like a human. They parse it. They extract. They need signals they can trust.

**51% of B2B buyers now start research in an AI chatbot.** When someone asks ChatGPT "best SEO agency for small business," the engine isn't running a keyword match — it's cross-referencing structured data signals across multiple sources. Businesses with complete LocalBusiness schema get cited. Businesses without it don't.

Three reasons schema is the highest-ROI GEO move:

1. **It's what AI engines actually read.** ClaudeBot, GPTBot, and PerplexityBot all prioritize structured data when deciding what to cite.
2. **It feeds Knowledge Graph panels.** Google, Bing, and Apple Maps all pull from schema for their business panels.
3. **It enables rich results.** FAQ schema can land you in "People Also Ask" boxes. Review schema puts star ratings in search results. These aren't just SEO wins — they're visibility signals that AI engines factor into citation decisions.

## The Schema Stacks That Move the Needle

Not all schema is created equal. Here's what actually drives AI citations:

**Stack 1: The Local Authority Stack**
```json
LocalBusiness + GeoCoordinates + PostalAddress + AggregateRating + Review
```
This tells AI engines: here's who we are, where we are, what we do, and what people think of us. It's the minimum viable GEO setup. If you're a local business and you don't have this, you're leaving citations on the table.

**Stack 2: The Content Authority Stack**
```json
Article + FAQ + BreadcrumbList + Organization
```
Every blog post, every service page, every guide should carry this stack. FAQ schema is especially powerful — it gives AI engines ready-made Q&A pairs to pull from when users ask questions.

**Stack 3: The Product Authority Stack**
```json
Product + Offer + AggregateRating + Review
```
For ecommerce. Product schema with price, availability, and reviews directly feeds shopping queries in ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google's AI Overviews.

## Getting Started with Schema Markup

You don't need a developer for basic schema. Here's what you can do today:

**1. Generate your core schema.** Use Google's Structured Data Markup Helper or Schema.org's generator. Start with LocalBusiness or Organization — one type, done right, is worth more than ten types done poorly.

**2. Validate it.** Paste your URL into Google's Rich Results Test or Schema.org's validator. Fix every error and warning. Invalid schema is worse than no schema — AI engines will ignore broken markup entirely.

**3. Add FAQ schema to your top 5 pages.** Identify the most common questions your customers ask. Add FAQPage schema with those questions and clear, 40-60 word answers. This is the single highest-impact GEO move for most businesses.

**4. Check your bots.txt.** Make sure you're not accidentally blocking GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, or BingBot. You'd be shocked how many sites block AI crawlers without realizing it. These bots can't see your schema — or anything else — if you've shut the door.

**5. Monitor and iterate.** Schema needs change. Google adds new types. AI engines evolve. Run a quarterly schema audit to catch drift.
